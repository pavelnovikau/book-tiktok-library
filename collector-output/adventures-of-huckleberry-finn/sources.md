## Retained causal chain

Pap’s custody and violence → Huck’s staged murder → Jim’s flight from threatened sale → mutual concealment → raft and intended Ohio route → fog, apology, threatened betrayal and protection → missed Cairo and collision → feud, death and reunion → involuntary travelling companions → theatrical and inheritance frauds → Jim’s sale → Huck’s torn letter → the Phelps escape and its actual consequences, in warned Deep only.

The setting’s slavery and racial injustice are retained in what happens: threatened sale, family separation, capture, ownership claims, chains, threatened lynching and treatment of Jim after he nurses Tom. No racial slurs are repeated for atmosphere. No invented speech is placed in quotation marks. The final will, Tom’s foreknowledge, Pap’s identity and Huck’s departure appear only in level 3. Global notes intentionally do not narrate those discoveries.

## Boundaries

This condensation omits episodes and much of the novel’s language. It does not reproduce the seven dialect distinctions in Twain’s Explanatory. Huck remains morally inconsistent; an apology or torn letter is not presented as a completed cure for his racism. Jim’s caregiving does not earn his legal freedom, and Tom’s final adventure is not turned into a rescue triumph. No account of Jim’s later family life or of Pap’s murderer is supplied.
