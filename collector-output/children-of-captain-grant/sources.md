# Jules Verne — The Children of Captain Grant

Research and analytical map established before screen authoring, 2026-10-31. Approved cumulative contract: 36 / 90 / 144, including level-1 cover and final. Audience: teen-14-plus. Default: RU / dark / Essential.

## Access and reading boundary

This is an original analytical companion, not an abridged translation or a chapter-by-chapter retelling. Project Gutenberg 2083 supplied a lawful, freely accessible complete digital file of the **historical Charles F. Horne-edited English version**. Its 66 chapter headings were inventoried; selected passages across all three parts were read, including the principal clue, character, survival, education, and final-resolution passages. This is **not a claim of continuous full-book reading**, nor of having read every line of the 70-chapter French original. Seven French chapter pages and the original contents were consulted through web access: I.II; II.XIX; III.XVI, XVII, XX, XXI, XXII. Detailed passages actually used were I.II, II.XIX, III.XVII, XXI, XXII.

Direct command-line retrieval of French pages returned HTTP 403 and was not bypassed; the lawful web-readable passages were used. The Centre international Jules Verne record was available as an indexed record; a full-page fetch timed out. Its publication dates are used only as bibliography. No translator is invented for PG2083. No modern Russian translation was used; both digest languages are original companion prose. No publisher art, historical illustrations, copied dialogue, or invented page locators are included.

## Plot / claim / theme map — prior to screens

- **Desire:** reunite a family after a disappearance; make a useful decision before certainty is available.
- **Obstacle:** a damaged multilingual distress message preserves a latitude but not a longitude. Plausible word completions can misdirect an entire voyage.
- **Narrative engine:** recover fragments → propose a place → cross actual terrain → encounter contrary evidence → revise the reading. This is the digest's analytical model, not a formal scientific method proclaimed by Verne.
- **Actors:** Mary and Robert supply personal stakes; Helena initiates the practical alternative to official refusal; Glenarvan funds and leads; John Mangles and the crew supply maritime skill; Paganel supplies geography and comic fallibility; Mac Nabbs supplies restrained scrutiny; Thalcave supplies indispensable local knowledge. Their capabilities do not collapse into a lone hero.
- **Early cases:** the shark and bottle; three languages; the distinction between known, conjectured, unknown; gonie read as Patagonia and the first geographical hypothesis; official refusal; Paganel's wrong ship and language; Andes crossing, condor rescue, water shortage, Robert's ride, flood and ombú; a revised Australian reading; Gordon/O'Moore's farm, initial testimony, land/sea coordination, railway incident; Toline's imperial school geography.
- **Interpretive consequences:** persistence means revising explanations without casually abandoning people; courage depends on skills and support; maps organize attention but carry assumptions; trust requires distinctions between identity, access, and truth.
- **Counter-readings:** coincidences and providence prevent the ending from proving a reliable search algorithm; rich benefactors make the expedition possible but do not establish a universal rescue system; sympathy for Europeans coexists with racial hierarchy and colonial ambition; geography lectures are part of the artistic design but are not a modern reference work.
- **Mental model:** hold the human commitment firmly, hold the explanation provisionally, and let people with different knowledge change the route.

## Late evidence — SPOILERS (authoring ledger only)

The Deep block uses PG Australia XVI–XIX and New Zealand I–XXI; French II.XIX and III.XVII, XX, XXI, XXII corroborate critical places. Ayrton is Ben Joyce and seeks the Duncan; the party's belief that the yacht has been lost is false. Halley's Macquarie is wrecked and its crew abandon the passengers. Robert helps the captives escape; a tabooed burial site and the fictional geothermal stratagem enable further flight. Paganel's letter says New Zealand instead of Australia; Austin follows it and detains Ayrton after attempted coercion. Ayrton's bargain concerns his own history, not current knowledge of Grant's whereabouts. Tabor/Maria Theresa's different chart names defeat the last word-completion attempt. Grant and two sailors are found; the island becomes Ayrton's place of exile with supplies. Mary and Mangles marry; Robert's maritime future and the renewed colonial project qualify the happy closure. Paganel's tattoo/marriage joke is discussed as an optional late coda only if used, not an event imported from an adaptation.

## Known edition traps / limits

- Use 37°11′ south consistently. The English file's punctuation sometimes looks like seconds; the French explicitly says minutes.
- Do not promote the initial reconstructed 7 June into the true wreck date. French III.XXI dates the wreck to the night of 26–27 June 1862; PG has inconsistent dates, including an erroneous 1865 in one explanatory parenthesis. The companion avoids that unnecessary date anchor.
- The parallel is a search constraint, not a literal track followed without port deviations: Cape Town coaling already departs from it.
- Tabor is a location in the novel's world. This digest makes no claim that a modern real island exists at the book's coordinates.
- The condor, sudden floods, animal attacks and controlled eruption are narrated as adventure episodes, never validated as zoology, hydrology or safe techniques.
- No evidence is drawn from film/television adaptations. No full-reading or independent-review certification is made.
