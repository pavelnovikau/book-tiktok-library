## Claim map established before screen rendering

- Desire: understandable actions for sleep, energy, focus and daily function.
- Problem: appealing mechanisms can become universal promises; protocols interact with constraints, care and each other.
- Author’s shortest thesis: combine foundational behaviors with goal-specific physiological tools, adapting to life. Supported by official book description, not inferred chapter coverage.
- Causal model: goal → proposed stimulus/mechanism → measured outcome → possible practical benefit. Each arrow requires its own evidence.
- Evidence: circadian light consensus; CDC sleep guidance and AASM insomnia guideline; WHO activity and diet guidance; fasting trials/reviews; breathwork trial plus independent review; cold/sauna reviews; NIH/NCCIH supplement evidence and safety.
- Consequence: prioritize feasible foundations; evaluate specific tools by outcomes and opportunity costs.
- Counterarguments: exact routines are not necessary to obtain general health benefits; markers are not clinical outcomes; association is not causation; combinations are not validated by component evidence.
- Limits: no personalized medical assessment, dosing, diagnosis or treatment substitution; variable populations and follow-up; pregnancy/illness/medicines can change decisions.
- Durable model: goal, evidence, limits, reconsideration. This four-part model is digest synthesis, not an attributed book quotation.
