## Synthesis and evidence discipline

Original analytical jobs include separating obedience from trust; classifying failures by rule, information and coordination; distinguishing epistemic from moral trust; reading a narrator as a situated witness; distinguishing behavior, identity and rights; and testing who can contest a protective decision. These are explicitly `synthesis`, not newly discovered book scenes or empirical findings. Modern classroom examples are counterfactuals, visibly introduced as such. A claimType of `book` can cite a publisher guide or review: it identifies whose fictional event/idea is reported, **not** a claim of direct full-text access.

Uncertainties kept open: whether a given robot has subjective experience; Byerley's identity; whether the final order is utopia or paternalism; whether early story mechanisms form a perfectly consistent technical system. No definitive answers are invented. Full-edition textual, sentence-level and translation fidelity remain outside this package's evidence boundary.
