# Meditations — source and quotation ledger

Access and verification: 2026-10-31. Work: Marcus Aurelius, *Τὰ εἰς ἑαυτόν*, twelve books; Russian title *Наедине с собой. Размышления*. This is an original thematic condensation of the personal notes, with 14 paired quotation excerpts. It is neither a complete translation nor a reconstruction of a chronological diary. No invented historical scenes, dialogue, motives, modern exercises, or quotations are used.

## What was read for this condensation

Selected primary places across all twelve books, including the full cited local arguments rather than a blurb: I.1–3,7,9,16–17; II.1–3,8,14; III.2–3; IV.3–5,17,20,24,33,40,49; V.1,9,20; VI.10,13,20–21,30,44,50,54; VII.9,22,48,54; VIII.36,47–48,59; IX.1,6,11,29,40; X.16; XI.18; XII.14,36 (last is printed XII.35 in Rogovin). VIII.48 was contextual reading, not a separate retained screen. Additional front matter and adjacent paragraphs were inspected for edition identity and numbering. The structured screen map below identifies exactly which material survived.

The thematic order begins with debts to other people, moves through waking, common reason, judgment, action and justice, then through fame, returning after failure and death. That arrangement is ours; the arguments, images, examples and self-address belong to Marcus. Each nested mode retains that arc. The 39-screen count comes from these distinct retained passages; 18/30/39 are exact cumulative counts, not length quotas.

## Quotation policy and numbering

All quotations are **excerpts**, not complete sections. Russian quotes were transcribed/check-read against the page images, with historical spelling converted to modern spelling (ѣ→е, і→и, final ъ removed, old inflection spelling modernized), line-wrap hyphens removed and whitespace normalized. Wording was not replaced with modern synonyms. Punctuation internal to the excerpt follows the scan; quotation boundaries and external quote marks are editorial. Some excerpts intentionally omit terminal source punctuation at their boundary. English excerpts preserve Gutenberg wording and internal punctuation; quotation marks and line wrapping are editorial. The surrounding English prose is an original paraphrase, not an uncredited modern translation.

**Concordance:** both languages use the same common book/section locator. In the chosen original Rogovin edition, XII.17 combines material Long numbers XII.17–18, shifting the final numbering. Therefore the final common locator **XII.36 = Rogovin 1914 XII.35**, explicitly shown in both reader attributions and the ledger. No one is asked to find a nonexistent Rogovin §36. Long's closing *satisfied* and Rogovin's *благожелательность* are preserved as the translators' different renderings; neither was rewritten to force a word-for-word match. No claim of completeness or superior accuracy of either translation is made.

## Limits and evidence artifacts

No essential retained causal or argumentative link remains unsupported by consulted primary places. This does not establish coverage of every subject or every section in the twelve books. We do not reconstruct Marcus's campaigns, private motives, exact writing dates, final living moments, or publication intentions from a modern blurb. The exercise collision and the actor are explicitly the text's examples/metaphors, not invented episodes. The condensation leaves out other passages; it does not claim they were all read or that quotation choices exhaust the important passages of the work.

No unattributed internet slogans (for example “You have power over your mind—not outside events”) are passed off as exact Long or Rogovin quotations. No Greek manuscript collation or complete translation-fidelity certification was performed. Modern orthography is disclosed rather than disguised as a photographic transcription. The original Rogovin scan resolves the earlier uncertainty attached to Wikisource's unverified modern witness. MIT's actual truncation remains disclosed.
