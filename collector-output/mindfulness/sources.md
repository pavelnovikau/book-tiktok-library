## Claim map prepared before screens

- Desire: inhabit ordinary life more fully without waiting for work, emotions or circumstances to become perfect.
- Problem: a useful task-solving system can apply discrepancy checking to feelings; mood, memory, bodily sensations and self-criticism then reinforce one another.
- Thesis: learn to recognize automatic doing and deliberately shift into direct, kind awareness; return to action with more choice.
- Mechanism: notice wandering → reorient to present sensation → allow rather than immediately eliminate experience → recognize thoughts as events → select an appropriate response. Doing remains useful.
- Evidence and examples: Lucy’s work-driven rumination; Alex’s bedtime/party autopilot; Hannah’s changing mental weather; Elsa’s struggle with body scan; approach/avoidance maze as the book’s illustration; rumor analogy; Elana Rosenbaum’s receiving help during cancer treatment; guilt/general memory; Marissa and Åsberg’s exhaustion funnel.
- Program: week 1 attention/autopilot; 2 body/sensing; 3 movement and breathing space; 4 sounds and thoughts; 5 manageable difficulty/acceptance; 6 befriending; 7 activity balance and action; 8 continued, personally meaningful practice. Habit releasers bring the same learning into ordinary life.
- Consequence: mindfulness is a way to relate to experience and choose action, not successful thought suppression or permanent pleasant feeling.
- Limits: self-help book ≠ clinician-led MBCT/MBSR; evidence depends on population, comparator and outcome; no guarantee, no zero-risk claim, no inference of cancer cure, no universal brain/immune/longevity benefit. Do not use acceptance to excuse danger or unfair conditions.
- Durable model: notice → anchor → allow → choose. This four-part wording is digest synthesis, not a quotation or validated protocol.

## Disagreements and uncertainties

The 2014 annotation and introductory book passages make broad therapeutic and no-side-effect claims. They are not repeated as established facts. NICE’s current NG222 recommendations and actual NHS service delivery concern structured therapy, professional assessment and relapse planning; they do not endorse this digest as treatment. The MBCT option in recommendation 1.8.5 is not a generic recommendation for anyone whose depression improved: it is framed for higher-risk people who remitted after antidepressants and are choosing whether to continue them, with other pathways handled separately. PREVENT tested adults with recurrent depression and supported tapering, not unsupported medication withdrawal. Its lack of superiority is not proof of universal equivalence. TAME concerned a specific intensive MBSR course, not this book; noninferiority does not mean identical effects for every participant. NCCIH reports heterogeneous evidence and incomplete harms ascertainment. No promised percentage reduction is generalized to the reader.

The book’s personal accounts, historical rumor anecdote, donkey/king parables, maze result, facial-feedback example and neuroscience claims are not independently re-verified here. Only selected clearly labeled stories are retained; no causal clinical conclusion rests on them. The facial-feedback result and broad brain, immunity, lifespan and hospital-use claims are excluded from the factual argument. Elana’s story concerns coping and receiving help during medical treatment, not a causal explanation of recovery. Kate’s crisis story is not retold as a self-help template. The favorable Publishers Weekly review supplies reception and structural triangulation only.

Research access limits: the publisher’s live page presented a loading screen; identifying search-index text and the local edition supplied bibliographic corroboration. NICE’s canonical recommendations were available through indexed text while one direct open returned 403; precise numbered recommendations were checked against the official indexed source and NHS delivery page. The NCCIH page, official author resource page, PREVENT abstract, TAME full article and Publishers Weekly review were opened. Source links are optional navigation; no network dependency is included in the reader.

No quotations, copied meditation scripts, poems, copyrighted image assets or full-text excerpts appear in the four deliverables.

Mechanism cross-check: current NICE material treats rumination as relevant to relapse risk; the 2026 primary neural analysis explicitly retains uncertainty about mechanisms. Memory specificity is cross-checked against independent quasi-experimental primary work, with its matched-control and non-randomized design made explicit on screen. Neither source is used to validate the entire proposed feedback loop, personal stories, or broader health claims.
