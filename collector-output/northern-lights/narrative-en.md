# Northern Lights — connected manuscript

Original retelling of the first novel. The complete plot and ending follow. Paragraphs were written before assigning mobile screens. This is not a reproduction of Pullman's prose.

Lyra Belacqua knows the roofs of Jordan College better than her lessons. She and her friend Roger would much rather explore the college than obey its adults. Always beside her is Pantalaimon, or Pan, her dæmon. In this Oxford a human soul has an animal shape, and children's dæmons can still change theirs. Lyra and Pan argue, but a life apart is unthinkable. One evening she slips into the Scholars' forbidden Retiring Room. Pan, disguised as an inconspicuous moth, wants to leave immediately. Lyra makes herself comfortable. Then she sees the Master pour white powder into wine intended for her formidable uncle, Lord Asriel. Leaving is no longer possible. Hidden in a wardrobe, she waits for Asriel and knocks the glass from his hand. He seizes her wrist painfully: gratitude must wait while she explains that she has saved his life. Then he tells her to hide again and keep an eye on the Master.

From the wardrobe Lyra watches Asriel's northern photograms. They show mysterious Dust and a city appearing among the lights of the aurora. Asriel wants money to investigate other worlds. The Scholars agree, but afterwards the Master tells the Librarian why he tried to stop him: he fears a predicted disaster in which Lyra has a part. She does not hear this conversation. For now, the North means adventure. Meanwhile children are disappearing. Stories of the Gobblers grow more frightening, and then Roger vanishes. Lyra searches for him, but instead of help she receives an unexpected invitation: the fascinating Mrs Coulter wants an assistant and tells her about northern expeditions. Before Lyra leaves, the Master secretly gives her an alethiometer, a golden instrument with symbols and needles that can tell the truth. How to read it remains a mystery. Mrs Coulter must not be shown the gift.

In London Mrs Coulter surrounds Lyra with softness, clothes and attention. Her golden monkey is nothing like cautious Pan. Soon coercion breaks through the affection: during an argument the monkey pins Pan to the floor, and Lyra herself feels his pain. At Mrs Coulter's party in London she overhears the adults. The Gobblers are no street legend: the General Oblation Board, a Church body, is behind the kidnappings, and Mrs Coulter runs it. Asriel, meanwhile, is a prisoner of the armoured bears in the North. Lyra and Pan slip out of the flat. But in the night streets two men catch her in a net. Help comes out of the darkness: gyptian boat people kill her attackers, and she recognises Tony Costa among her rescuers. His mother, Ma Costa, hides her on their boat. Ma Costa's younger son, Billy, has disappeared too. Roger is no longer Lyra's only tie to their sorrow.

Gyptian families gather in the fens. Their leader John Faa refuses to surrender Lyra despite the large reward for her and prepares an expedition to recover the stolen children. From him she learns that Asriel is her father and Mrs Coulter her mother. Ma Costa nursed her as a baby; the adults around her have kept a history she herself never knew. While the men prepare, Lyra entertains the children with increasingly extravagant stories about Asriel. John Faa, however, still refuses to take her. Farder Coram helps her explore the alethiometer. Concentrating on its symbols, she learns of a gyptian spy's death before the news arrives. Now the expedition needs her gift. Mechanical spy-insects follow: one is caught, another escapes. Mrs Coulter may discover where they are. At last the waiting ends in a sea voyage north.

Aboard ship Pan becomes a dolphin and plays among real dolphins. Lyra laughs, then worries: what if he likes that life better than he likes her? He cannot swim far away from her all the same. Trollesund comes into view: mountains, wooden houses, a harbour, the smell of fish and cold air from the land. Here Farder Coram visits the witches' consul: long ago he saved one of their number, Serafina Pekkala. The consul tests Lyra's gift—she identifies the right spray of cloud-pine—and advises them to hire an armoured bear. Out of her hearing he tells Coram about an old prophecy: the girl must do something important without knowing her role. Lyra herself is busy with nearer wonders. The bear Iorek Byrnison works for humans in exchange for food and drink. The townspeople got him drunk and took his armour. Without it he will not leave. At night Kaisa, Serafina's goose dæmon, arrives, promising help towards Bolvangar, where the children are held.

Lyra finds where the armour is hidden and tells Iorek. The Texan aeronaut Lee Scoresby helps her, distracting the adults with talk of cards while his dæmon carries a message to the girl. With his armour restored, Iorek becomes so terrifying that the town's guards can no longer hold him to his former life. The bear joins the expedition. The party takes sledges into the snow. Iorek carries Lyra across the white wilderness as she continues to question the alethiometer. Once it points towards a nearby village: something dreadful is there, something like a child or a ghost. Lyra persuades John Faa to let her investigate with Iorek.

In a shed sits a boy without a dæmon. His name is Tony Makarios; instead of his missing Ratter he clutches a piece of fish. Lyra's first response is horror, but she goes to him. Iorek helps bring him back to the party, where he is fed and warmed. It is no use: Tony dies. Lyra scratches his dæmon's name onto a gold coin so that he can have at least that semblance of a bond with Ratter. Now the disappearances reveal a particular horror: the Gobblers have been cutting away children's dæmons. On the journey Iorek also explains his exile: he killed another bear. Lyra hopes the bears of Svalbard can be outwitted so that she can reach Asriel. Iorek lets her try to deceive him in a playful fencing match. Whatever feint she attempts, he sees the move coming.

An attack changes everything. Samoyed hunters descend on the party, seize Lyra and sell her to Bolvangar. She calls herself Lizzie Brooks and acts quiet and dull. She even hugs a rag doll, though dolls have never interested her. Inside are routines, measurements, examinations and children who do not know when they will next be called away. Here Lyra finally finds Roger and Billy Costa. But Mrs Coulter is coming soon. If she recognises Lyra, there will be no more hiding. During a fire drill Lyra starts a snowy commotion, then slips away with the boys to explore the forbidden buildings. Kaisa arrives with news that the rescuers are near. He and Lyra discover cages of severed dæmons. Kaisa helps free them and takes them away. Lyra arranges a signal with the children: at the next alarm they must seize their warm clothes and run. The engine of Mrs Coulter's zeppelin is already audible above Bolvangar.

Lyra hides above the ceiling and listens to doctors discussing their dæmon-separating machine. They discover her. She fights, but one doctor takes Pan in his bare hands, violating an appalling taboo, and Lyra can scarcely resist. They are placed on opposite sides of the separator. Mrs Coulter intervenes. This time the woman in charge of experiments on children will not let the same thing happen to her daughter. She soothes Lyra, promising that after the operation a dæmon can become a wonderful pet. Lyra plays along. In place of the alethiometer she offers the tin containing the captured spy-insect. Mrs Coulter opens it and is attacked by her own instrument of surveillance. Lyra gets away.

Lyra starts a fire in the kitchen and sets off the alarm. The children rush out towards the guards and their wolf dæmons. Snowballs fly at the slits in the guards' helmets, but they are little use against rifles. Witches' arrows strike from the darkness, Iorek plunges into the fighting, and Lyra leads the children towards the gyptians. Even here Mrs Coulter tries to drag her away again. Roger goes to her aid; Serafina lifts them both to Scoresby's balloon. Iorek climbs aboard too. The other children remain with their rescuers, while the balloon travels onwards to Svalbard. Lyra is sure she must help her father and bring him the alethiometer.

In flight she talks with Serafina. To Lyra, Farder Coram is an old man; the witch remembers him young. They loved each other and had a son, lost to an epidemic. Serafina lives for centuries while Coram has grown old. She also tells Lyra about Iorek: he is high-born, whatever his exile has made of his life. Later, cliff-ghasts attack the balloon. Amid the violent jolts and collisions Lyra falls out of the basket; below, Svalbard's bears find her. She expected to arrive among them with Iorek. Instead she is their prisoner, alone.

In the dungeon Lyra learns more about King Iofur Raknison. More than anything he wants a dæmon, like a human's. An absurd doll sits on the enormous bear's lap in place of the companion he does not have. Lyra wins an audience and announces that she is Iorek's dæmon, created at Bolvangar. Naturally she would prefer to serve such a great king as Iofur. But she can only become his if he defeats Iorek in single combat. Secret knowledge from the alethiometer helps her withstand the king's tests. She has secured a duel for her friend instead of death at the hands of all the guards. Seeing Iofur ready to fight frightens her: he is larger and stronger, and Iorek is exhausted by his journey. Iorek does not blame her. This is exactly the fight he wanted. For her dangerously persuasive words he gives her a new name, Lyra Silvertongue.

The bears tear armour and flesh. Iorek weakens; Iofur taunts him. But he is deceived by his opponent—the very thing Iorek said a true bear should recognise. Iorek kills him and becomes king. The human decorations begin to come off the palace. Roger finds her again. The friends hurry towards Asriel: Mrs Coulter is approaching, and Lyra still believes it is the instrument her father needs.

Instead of a wretched captive, they find a man who has arranged a warm, well-equipped laboratory in the ice. Asriel is horrified to see his daughter and orders her away. Then he notices Roger, changes his manner and lets them both in. His look frightens Roger. Lyra tells her father that she knows their relationship, but receives none of the warmth she wants. Asriel speaks of Dust, original sin and his intention to destroy the source of evil and death. His destination is the world beyond the aurora. He tells Lyra to keep the alethiometer. Only after waking to learn that he has left with Roger does she understand her mistake. What he needed was a child. She herself has brought him the friend she crossed the North to save.

Lyra races after them on Iorek. Witches from the other side and Mrs Coulter's zeppelin attack the bears. They bring the zeppelin down, but ahead is a bridge that cannot take Iorek's weight. Lyra and Pan go on alone. On the heights Asriel prepares his experiment while his snow leopard holds Roger's dæmon. Lyra tries to free her friend. For a moment they get away, but the leopard captures the dæmon again and the ice beneath the children gives way. Roger dies in Lyra's arms. The breaking of the bond releases the energy Asriel wanted. The sky opens; the city in the lights becomes a world that can be reached.

Lyra sees Asriel embrace Mrs Coulter and invite her to join him. She refuses. He crosses the opening, leaving Lyra with what has happened to Roger. She and Pan decide to find the source of Dust before Asriel can and stop him. Together with her dæmon she steps into another world.
