## Reading and acquisition boundary

A read-only search of the assigned title/author in Apple Books filenames and EPUB title/creator metadata found no matching package. The script inspected 216 OPF metadata records, including expanded `.epub` directories. It printed no unrelated filenames and modified no library files. The sources below supply substantial **actual book text**, not just blurbs. The University of Washington course page publicly links chapters 1 and 9; the CSUN course page publicly links chapter 4. These are bounded institutional teaching excerpts. Source PDFs were fetched into memory, and selected image pages were OCR-read with Tesseract. No source PDF, scanned page or raw OCR transcript is stored in this collector or public repository. Fetch hashes and PDF extents are in `qa/source-fetch-evidence.json`. A PDF fetch is not a claim to continuous full-book reading.

This is the strongest source for the retained arc: formal/informal dependency; Scott's short return to Soviet and Tanzanian farming; work to rule; the meaning and local specificity of métis; pilotage; Mat Isa; adaptability, unequal access and the qualified defense of scientific/state simplifications. - **High modernism:** checked the Scott excerpts reproduced by the Schumacher Center, separating author paragraphs from the site's editorial preface. It is an excerpt, not the whole chapter 3. - **Four conditions:** Yale's publisher description states all four. Scott's own Introduction pp. 4–5 formulation was additionally checked as a direct, explicitly attributed quotation reproduced on p. 297 of Xueguang Zhou's institutional author-hosted article. The original full Introduction was not read. Zhou's later Chinese road-project cases and his own subsequent analysis are not presented as Scott's 1998 examples.

The retained Soviet/Tanzanian account is intentionally bounded to Scott's own chapter-9 recapitulation (p. 310), rather than reconstructing the full chapters 6–7 from memory. No relocation totals, death counts, village names, precise project chronology or comprehensive agronomic diagnosis is asserted. Chapter 8's full agriculture argument, the Lenin/Luxemburg chapter and the final chapter's stepwise policy prescriptions are not reconstructed. None is required to understand the retained sequence from simplification through coercion, lived cases and practical knowledge.

Late material was kept separate. Scott's 2010 Cato essay and 2010 Theory Talk interview were inspected during discovery; neither supplies a retained screen claim. No case from *The Art of Not Being Governed* (2009) or *Against the Grain* (2017), no later lecture anecdote and no modern management analogy is imported into the target book.

## Sufficiency judgment and limits

The retained chain has direct support for the key transition from **selection in representation** to **enforced transformation**, for genuine initial gains and delayed losses, for a planned-city case with its humane aspirations and lived contradictions, for the brief agricultural comparison in the author's own recap, and for **métis plus qualifications**. The four-element conjunction has checked author wording, although accessed indirectly. These sources jointly support this concise retained arc without pretending to verify every chapter claim.

No particular missing causal link in this retained arc was identified in the authoring self-check. Independent review may challenge either source sufficiency or the compression; readiness is a request for that review, not a fidelity certification. The historical/ecological claims are Scott's account, not independently replicated findings. No independent translation certification, whole-edition fidelity, full apparatus review or continuous complete-book reading is claimed.

No purchase, loan, credential request, subscription change, unofficial full-book retrieval or access bypass occurred. Original summaries and source locators are the only book content saved here; the complete copyrighted work is not distributed.
