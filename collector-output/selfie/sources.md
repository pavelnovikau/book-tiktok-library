## Argument before screens

Reader's question: why does a culture promising unlimited personal possibility make ordinary imperfection feel like failure of the whole person?

Central thesis: the status-seeking, story-making social self encounters culturally inherited standards of a perfectible individual. Western institutions progressively relocate agency, merit and blame into that individual; digital platforms make the resulting comparison and punishment unusually visible and continuous. A person can mistake a culturally selected ideal for their natural obligation.

Causal chain: evolved belonging/status needs → reputational learning and self-narration → local economic/social arrangements favour different selves → Greek agency and perfectibility → Christian inward moral struggle → therapeutic discovery of an allegedly good authentic self → self-esteem as policy and competitive individualism → digitally scored identity → perfectionistic self-discrepancy, distress and social punishment. This is Storr's historical synthesis, not a single experimentally demonstrated causal chain.

Evidence/cases: Debbie Hampton and researchers Rory O'Connor/Gordon Flett; John Pridmore's changed tribe; Gazzaniga and confabulation; Nisbett's cultural comparisons; Pluscarden Abbey; Freud/Perls/Rogers/Esalen; Ayn Rand/Nathaniel Branden/John Vasconcellos/Neil Smelser; Baumeister and Twenge with Trzesniewski's objections; Engelbart/Stewart Brand/Rainbow Mansion; CJ as an expressly extreme interview case; Austen Heinz with family disagreement over diagnosis; Brian Little/Daniel Nettle and personal projects.

Consequences: personal improvement is not an unlimited mandate; dignity differs from superiority; supporting someone differs from flattering them; institutions and environments cannot be replaced by positive self-talk; acceptance should preserve responsibility for harm. Durable model: temperament + situation + inherited ideal + feedback shape the self-story; a mismatch is information, not a verdict on a whole human being.

Counterarguments and boundaries: individualism has enabled genuine freedom and innovation; collectivism is not a cure; traditions and nations are internally diverse; correlation is not intervention evidence; self-esteem is not narcissism; high standards are not identical to pathological perfectionism; a case is not a population estimate; suicide is multiply determined; personality stability is not immutability. Later research on self-esteem is more positive than a sloganized reading of the book. Post-2017 corroboration is explicitly separated from the text.
