## Claim map — completed before screen drafting

Central question: who can decide for others while keeping gains and exporting losses, and what does this do to knowledge, ethics and collective survival?

Thesis: the relevant stake is exposure to the adverse consequences of decisions, not merely investment, enthusiasm, profit sharing or willingness to gamble. Symmetry is simultaneously an ethical constraint and a selection mechanism.

Causal backbone: insulated agent → hidden tail exposure → favourable short record and private rewards → delayed damage elsewhere → no removal of the harmful agent → accumulated fragility. Shared downside can reconnect action and reality; it cannot by itself prove truth or guarantee a good institution.

1. Four lenses: reliable knowledge, justice/reciprocity, information and dynamic rationality. `ch1-3.xhtml`.
2. Antaeus, interventions, Rubin and learning through elimination. `ch1-4.xhtml#id2`–`#id7`; technical transfer-of-harm argument `ch1-33.xhtml#id199`.
3. Hammurabi, silver versus golden rule, local reciprocity and scale. `ch1-6.xhtml#id9`–`#id12`; `ch1-7.xhtml#id13`–`#id16`. Ancient sanctions illustrate exposure, not acceptable modern punishment.
4. Simplicity, legal versus ethical, skin versus soul, artisans and entrepreneurial risk imposed on families. `ch1-8.xhtml#id17`–`#id21`; `ch1-9.xhtml#id22`–`#id29`.
5. Agency, turtles, information disclosure, repeated exchange and conflict of interest; badly designed medical metrics move rather than remove asymmetry. Chapter 1, `ch1-12.xhtml#id37`–`#id48`.
6. Minority rule: asymmetric acceptance sets, distributed inflexible actors, aggregation/renormalization, accommodation cost and geography. Chapter 2, `ch1-14.xhtml#id50`–`#id65`. No universal percentage, moral endorsement or proof that every minority wins.
7. Scale: the group is not an average individual; segregation, institutional constraints and zero-intelligence traders. End of chapter 2, `ch1-14.xhtml`. The trader result is corroborated only within the original model's constraints.
8. Employment buys reliability through dependence; freedom has costs; relatives can become hostages. Chapters 3–4, `ch1-16.xhtml#id67`–`#id78`; `ch1-17.xhtml#id79`–`#id83`. Dependence can prevent truth-telling, which complicates any universal celebration of stakes.
9. Simulation, embodied life and the intellectual-yet-idiot polemic. Chapters 5–6, `ch1-18.xhtml#id85`–`#id97`. Critique of insulated expertise does not disprove expertise as such.
10. Dynamic versus static inequality; turnover, rent and protected upside. Chapter 7, `ch1-19.xhtml#id98`–`#id107`. Descriptive mobility and normative fairness remain different questions.
11. Lindy: time and exposure as selectors of nonperishable practices; limits of survival as validation. Chapter 8, `ch1-20.xhtml#id108`–`#id119`. Independent review raises survival of the very institutions Taleb criticizes.
12. Surgeon selection, green lumber, polished plans, luxury/overcomplication, deeds, news and virtue signalling. Chapters 9–13, `ch1-22.xhtml#id121`–`#id140`, `ch1-23.xhtml#id141`, `ch1-24.xhtml#id147`, `ch1-25.xhtml#id152`. Conditional thought experiments remain conditional; appearance is not a qualification test.
13. Peace, commerce, selection of dramatic historical episodes; religious practice, sacrifice and embodied divinity. Chapters 14–17, `ch1-26.xhtml#id160`, `ch1-27.xhtml#id166`–`#id175`. Costly commitment is evidence of commitment, not evidence that a proposition is true.
14. Survival-based rationality, ensemble versus time, ruin, repeated exposure, nested selves, courage and precaution. Chapters 18–19 and epilogue, `ch1-29.xhtml#id177`, `ch1-30.xhtml#id186`–`#id195`; appendix `ch1-33.xhtml`. Ecological/global extrapolations remain contested; not a general veto on innovation.
15. Digest synthesis: trace decision, reward, delayed downside, removal, scale and powerless third parties. Objections: moral luck, coercion, distorted metrics, selection bias, selection at another group's expense, expertise and impracticable literal symmetry. Keep these distinguishable from Taleb's own qualifications.

Essential must carry the complete argument, including minority conditions, scale, dependence, Lindy objection, rationality/ruin, responsibility limits and the final transferable model. Core inserts cases/mechanisms; Deep inserts secondary examples and bounded objections. No level is a separate summary.

## Unresolved / excluded assertions

No independently validated numerical threshold for minority dominance. No universal statistical Lindy law. No proof that surviving customs are good, that historians systematically overstate every war, that GMOs entail global ruin, or that a medical treatment should be refused. No speculation that contemporary events vindicate Taleb. Illustrative growth arithmetic in the digest is explicitly its own stipulated example, not a quotation, experiment or investment forecast.
