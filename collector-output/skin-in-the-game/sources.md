## What this condensed version does

It follows Taleb's connecting argument: downside and selection; reciprocity and exchange; asymmetric minority choice; dependence; practical knowledge and appearances; words versus deeds; religion as practice; survival and ruin. It uses the author's actual stories rather than invented applications. It retains attribution where geopolitical, medical and technological judgments could otherwise read as established independent conclusions.

This is not a chapter-complete representation or a whole-book fidelity certificate. Omissions include much named-person polemic, numerical claims about politics, replication and inequality, extended assassin anecdotes, some religious comparisons and technical derivations. Source-grounded compression is deliberate; no claim of exhaustive coverage or independent medical/historical validation is made. No outside reviewer or later research substitutes for Taleb in the reading sequence.
