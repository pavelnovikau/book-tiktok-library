# The Art of War — source ledger and claim map

Access date: 2026-10-31. Artifact: original analytical paraphrase in Russian and English, not an edition or a translation from Classical Chinese. The full thirteen-chapter Lionel Giles text (1910) is the primary reading base; the separate annotated edition is used only where explicitly identified. No publisher images or edition prose are reproduced. No direct quotations are used in the screens. Short chapter names and technical terms identify the object of discussion.

## Evidence boundary

The complete public-domain base text was consulted, including chapters XII–XIII. Selected notes and introduction in the annotated Giles edition were consulted, not every historical anecdote. Modern scholarship was consulted through the public Bowdoin textual apparatus, Morillo's abstract, Columbia's Mair description and contents, Boorman's chapter summary, the opening of Lorge's official ten-page excerpt, and the Cambridge historical chapter summary. Paywalled books/chapters were not read in full. Their advertised arguments are attributed to their named scholars, never passed off as a scholarly consensus. A local Smart Reading condensation was not consulted or used as the book; it supplies no claims here. No historical author, exact composition date, or sixth-century biography is treated as established. Yinqueshan is an early witness, not an autograph or a complete surviving book.

## Claim map established before screen writing

- Desire/question: understand what prudent judgment looks like when conflict can consume the very community it is meant to protect.
- Problem: victories are easy to mistake for success; courage, numbers, speed, and confident prediction can conceal costs, coordination failures, and ignorance.
- Thesis: assess conditions and political costs, preserve capacity, shape a favorable situation, adapt to the other side, and obtain situated human knowledge; combat is a costly means, never a sufficient end.
- Causal chain: comparative assessment (I) → resource depletion (II) → preservation and limits on battle (III) → prior security (IV) → collective configuration (V) → relational advantage (VI) → friction and coordination (VII) → contingent judgment (VIII) → observation and trust (IX) → terrain plus command responsibility (X) → situation-dependent cohesion and coercion (XI) → irreversible loss and restraint (XII) → human intelligence and its price (XIII).
- Evidence: the treatise's own examples, numbers, and metaphors establish its argument, not validated military laws. Selected later commentary illustrates reception, not events witnessed by Sunzi.
- Consequence: competent leadership depends on systems, costs, information, restraint, and responsibility rather than spectacular action alone.
- Qualifications: coercion and deception remain real; preserving an enemy whole is not pacifism; probabilistic modern validation is absent; text and translations vary; management transfer is a limited analogy, not ancient authorization.
- Durable model: purpose and irreversible cost; own condition; relation to circumstances; quality of knowledge; explicit stopping conditions. This five-question arrangement is the digest's synthesis.

2. **Lionel Giles, Sun Tzŭ on The Art of War, introduction and critical notes (1910)**, Project Gutenberg #132. https://www.gutenberg.org/files/132/132-h/132-h.htm — `reference`. Selected introduction/textual-history passages and notes at I.4–10, V.3–23, VIII title, XI.50, XIII.26–27. Separates Sunzi's base text, Cao Cao / Du Mu and other later commentators, and Giles's editorial judgments. Historical anecdotes in these notes are not contemporary evidence for the ancient text.
3. **Stephen Morillo, “Sun Zi (Sun Tzu) (ca. fourth century BCE)” (2011)**, The Encyclopedia of War, Wiley. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/9781444338232.wbeow613 — `reference`. Public abstract consulted. Supports uncertainty over Sun Wu's existence, traditional dating around 500 BCE versus later compilation; does not prove a precise date.
4. **Kidder Smith / Denma Translation Group, scholarly apparatus: Overview**, Bowdoin College. https://learn.bowdoin.edu/suntzu/overview.html — `reference`. Full public overview consulted. Fragmentary second-century-BCE Yinqueshan witness, comparison with received text, and a chapter IV variant reversing the relation of attack/defense and sufficiency. Its dating description is not treated as an exact copying date. This is the Denma editors' argued reading, not a claim that all scholars agree.
5. **Victor H. Mair, The Art of War: Sun Zi's Military Methods**, Columbia University Press (2007; paperback 2009). https://cup.columbia.edu/book/the-art-of-war/9780231133838/ — `publisher`. Public description and contents consulted, not the complete translation. Supports alternate chapter naming and Mair's specific hypothesis of compilation from mid-fourth to early-third century BCE, including rejection of Sun Wu's historicity. These are attributed positions. A publisher-linked appendix returned 404 and was not used.
6. **Scott Boorman, “Background: Historical and Textual,” Three Faces of Sun Tzu (2024)**, Cambridge University Press. https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/three-faces-of-sun-tzu/background-historical-and-textual/A73F375D8A28B97EDB6277495D663D1A — `reference`. Public chapter summary consulted. Accretionist perspective, multiple textual witnesses, eleven traditional commentators, and distinction between novelistic reception and textual tradition. Full chapter not accessed.
7. **Peter Lorge, Sun Tzu in the West, Introduction**, official Cambridge excerpt. https://assets.cambridge.org/97811088/30652/excerpt/9781108830652_excerpt.pdf — `reference`. ## Reading and quotation policy

All screens are new RU/EN prose. Technical terms (dao, shi, zheng/qi) and chapter names are labels, not quotations. No long quotations, modern translation passages, publisher art, or fabricated page references. URLs are optional evidence links; the reader itself is fully offline. Numerical examples retain the source's units and are marked as textual illustrations, with no modern currency/distance conversion.
