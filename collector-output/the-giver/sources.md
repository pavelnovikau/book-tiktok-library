# The Giver / Дающий — sources and evidence boundary

Access date: 2026-10-31. This is an original concise RU/EN retelling of Lois Lowry’s **The Giver (1993), the first novel**. It does not adapt the film, graphic novel or later books. RU is new prose, not a claim to reproduce any published Russian translation.

This working file contains plot information for the independent reviewer. The reader’s global Sources dialog uses shorter, spoiler-safe notes instead.

## Edition and actual reading

The original novel is identified in Scholastic’s bibliography as *The Giver*, New York: Houghton Mifflin Co., 1993. The authorised novel excerpts in source 2 identify their page references as the hardcover **ISBN 978-0-547-99566-3**. Only those passages, not that entire hardcover, were consulted. Source 4 is a different format: Listening Library audiobook download, ISBN **9780553751376**, published July 5, 2000, 4h 48m. The audiobook was not listened to and must not be listed as a continuously read print edition.

No matching owned title/author was found in the supplied Apple Books root. Both expanded EPUB OPFs and ZIP EPUB OPFs were considered; the final exact title/creator filter inspected 218 metadata candidates and returned zero matching editions. Consequently there was no target OPF/spine/body to inspect for completeness and no basis for claiming either a full owned novel or a SmartReading condensation. Unrelated books were not used.

No complete edition was read continuously. Retained scenes are supported by a substantial publisher guide, selected permission-labelled novel passages, a second official discussion guide and Lowry’s own commentary. The scene-by-scene evidence map is [source-evidence-map.json](source-evidence-map.json). Guide summaries and teacher prompts are explicitly distinguished from actual novel excerpts.

## Sources used in the reader

1. **Jeannette Sanderson, Scholastic BookFiles: A Reading Guide to The Giver** (Scholastic, 2003; ISBN 0-439-46356-4). [Official PDF](https://www.scholastic.com/content/dam/teachers/activities/migrated-files-in-body/the-giver-bookfile.pdf). Download verified: HTTP 200, PDF, 64 physical pages, 278891 bytes. Consulted printed pp. 12–14 (Lowry interview), 18–29 (chapter questions and connected plot), 30–32 (setting), 33–46 (themes and characters); selected glossary entry on p. 50 and bibliography reference on p. 63. This is a reading guide, not a complete novel. Its plot narrative supports the chronology, Gabriel’s care, apple/Fiona, training, difficult memories, recorded release, escape plan, interrupted preparation, journey and final perceptions. Some short novel quotations are embedded in the guide; they do not establish continuous chapter reading.

2. **Robert L. Selman and Tracy Elizabeth, The Giver: Educator’s Resource Guide** (2014). [Permission-labelled PDF](https://www.reellifewithjane.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/The-Giver-Educators-Resource-Guide.pdf). Verified HTTP 200, 28 physical pages, 2414506 bytes. The credit page states that novel excerpts are copyright 1993 Lois Lowry, used with Houghton Mifflin Harcourt permission, and names hardcover ISBN 9780547995663. Actual primary passages read: guide p. 9 / novel pp. 70–71 (chapter 9, rule permitting lies); guide p. 13 / novel pp. 122–123 (chapter 13, wanting choices); guide p. 14 / novel pp. 159–160 (chapter 16, asking parents about love); guide p. 19 / novel pp. 195–196 (chapter 20, the Giver staying to help the community and Jonas’s ashamed response). Also consulted book-specific notes on guide pp. 12–14 and 17–19; in particular p. 18’s explicitly book-linked note on hiding from heat-seeking planes, novel pp. 211–212. Those notes are secondary, not additional chapter text. The PDF mixes book and film material: screenplay excerpts, film teaching activities and imagined student scenes were excluded. The current Walden URL was 404; the licensed guide was obtained from the accessible mirror, not relabelled as a current Walden-hosted download.

3. **Lois Lowry, Newbery Acceptance Speech, June 1994**. [Actual author-site PDF](https://loislowry.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/the_giver.pdf), reached from [Speeches](https://loislowry.com/speeches/). Verified HTTP 200, nine PDF pages, 19997 bytes; read across the document. It contains a brief river passage and the author’s account of the book’s origins; PDF pp. 7–8 discuss readers’ different endings without selecting a single correct interpretation. This supports restraint at the ending, not an extra fictional scene. The author’s autobiographical episodes were not inserted into Jonas’s story. The older `/pdf/Newbery_Award.pdf` route was 404.

4. **Penguin Random House, The Giver listing and A Conversation with Lois Lowry**. [Current publisher page](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/104094/the-giver-by-lois-lowry/). Verified HTTP 200. Read the description, edition metadata and author interview. The interview directly supports the hasty change of departure plans, food/bicycle/baby and the aim of saving the baby. Lowry’s personal optimism about the ending is not converted into a definite survival outcome, and her comments about companion books are not used as first-novel plot facts.

5. **Gary D. Schmidt, The Giver Reader’s Guide, Penguin Random House**. [Reading Guide](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/104094/the-giver-by-lois-lowry/readers-guide/), followed from the current publisher page rather than assumed. Verified HTTP 200; read questions 1–8 and attribution. The guide checks community arrangements, the euphemism around release and the existence of conflicting interpretations of the last scene. It is secondary discussion, not a primary passage and not a substitute for reading every chapter.

## Retained scope and limits

The main chain is source-supported: unusual appointment → memories of a wider world → altered relationships → the recording → a plan whose preparation is overtaken by Gabriel’s danger → departure and pursuit → hunger/cold → the last descent. These are events and conflicts in the story, not lessons for the reader.

Essential/Core stop before the recording is shown. Deep alone includes Gabriel receiving memories, the earlier trainee’s returned memories, what release means, the plan, danger/departure and final scene. The final passage reports the sled, lights and singing as Jonas’s experience; it does not establish a confirmed rescue, death or transformed community. Later-book facts are excluded.

Omitted rather than expanded from memory: the earlier trainee’s name, family revelation and detailed fate; the needle’s anatomical site and disposal details; the full training schedule; specific dream/medication scenes; invented private motives; film romance, injections, sensor tricks or a physical barrier; distances, a mapped route and any epilogue. The selected passages do not warrant certifying the entire novel’s prose, every chapter or every omitted relationship. They do support this deliberately limited retained sequence, subject to independent content review.

Only original paraphrase, locators and metadata are in this package. No complete copyrighted edition, copied chapter, private EPUB or source extract is included. Private research downloads/extracted text remain outside the public repository. HTML embeds no copyrighted cover image.
