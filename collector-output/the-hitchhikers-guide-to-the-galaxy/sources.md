## Access boundary

The complete novel was **not read**, downloaded or acquired. No full-text loan was activated; no unauthorized full-text results were opened. No local complete edition is claimed or required for this analytical commission. The digest does not reproduce dialogue, narrative prose, lyrics, publisher artwork, or a published Russian translation.

“Book” means a reported fictional event, object or character trait supported by the identified excerpt or secondary event evidence, not a claim that every page was collated. “Corroboration” identifies external context or a critic’s position. “Synthesis” identifies this digest’s own reading, comparison or question; citations give its factual starting point rather than attributing the inference to Adams. In-world technology is imaginary, not an engineering, medical or survival instruction. Comic violence, drinking, coercion and a machine’s self-destruction are discussed as fictional material, never recommendations to a young reader.

The source bundle can sustain 96 distinct analytical jobs, but cannot support an edition-complete retelling, full textual analysis, or exact chapter/page references after the opening excerpt. Late-event detail is intentionally narrow and described below. External links can themselves spoil the ending.

## Claim / plot / theme map (recorded before screen drafting)

Question: What remains worth doing when the world stops fitting your explanation of it?

Working reading, explicitly a synthesis rather than an author manifesto: the novel strips ordinary institutions and cosmic explanations of automatic authority. Arthur’s survival depends on fallible companions, while the reader learns to distinguish explanation, reassurance, status and care. Humour makes this inquiry possible without supplying a final universal formula.

Causal spine: threatened home → familiar administrative reasoning at planetary scale → displaced observer and experienced guide → unreliable knowledge and mismatched personalities → fictional machinery makes luck visible → speculative planet-making enlarges questions about value → **Deep only:** promised cosmic answer fails to specify a question; Earth’s hidden role reframes the opening; research becomes coercion; escape ends the immediate danger without settling the meaning of life.

Planned nested architecture: twelve blocks of eight screens, each with two Essential, three additional Core and three additional Deep screens. The cover is the first Essential screen; the final is the last. Essential therefore has 24, Core 60, Deep 96. The first Deep insertion is an explicit spoiler notice. Major late revelations and the resolution occur only in Deep. Core discusses Magrathea’s planet-making premise but withholds Earth’s role, the numerical answer, the mice’s identity and demands, and the escape outcome.

Counterreadings: Kropf describes mock SF and denied closure; Woodford disputes the inference that life is therefore worthless. Adams’s own account says science fiction lets him satirize other things, challenging the reduction of his work to genre parody. Bracksieck’s educational interpretation is optimistic; it does not establish clinical efficacy or that Arthur attains a completed philosophy. The digest preserves these distinctions.

2. **Douglas Adams — “Introduction: A Guide to the Guide”.** [Publisher-hosted excerpt](https://www.penguinrandomhouseretail.com/book/?isbn=9780345453747). Author; only the introductory essay was consulted, **not the omnibus novels**. Locators: opening caution about conflicting histories; Innsbruck/title-origin passage; radio commissioning; publishers asking for the manuscript; final publication paragraph. Supports remembered genesis and medium history. Its September 1979 date differs from Pan’s October; only 1979 is asserted. No advice to copy the author’s drinking or deadline habits.
3. **Daniel Coston — Douglas Adams interview, November 1996, republished 2011.** [Interviewer’s page](https://danielcoston.blogspot.com/2011/09/douglas-adams-interview-1996.html). Author interview; read the interview, not just search snippets. Locators: questions “The ideas for ‘Hitchhiker’ came from several different inspirations”; “Ford Prefect … anti-Doctor Who”; “Why is that?” after destroying Earth; “Did you ever tire … working on next?”; final Internet-groups answer. Supports Adams’s description of using SF for social satire, Ford as an imperfect saviour and the cost of losing the Earth as reference point. The introductory misdescription of Zaphod as a Guide researcher and its date for Mostly Harmless are not adopted. Author recollection is not neutral plot verification.
4. **Jean Menzies / Pan Macmillan — The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy books: a complete guide (2024).** [Direct page](https://www.panmacmillan.com/blogs/science-fiction-and-fantasy/hitchhikers-guide-to-the-galaxy-book-series). Publisher; read series-order, first-novel and 42 sections. Locators: “How many books”; first title’s synopsis; “What does the 42 mean”; “What is the answer to life, the universe and everything”. Supports novel/radio distinction, premise, crew, towels, fish, poetry and question/answer mismatch. Later-book summaries were consulted only to enforce exclusion, not incorporated as first-novel events. The page’s dated streaming prediction and oversimplified claim that the sequel finally uncovers the question are excluded.
5. **Jonathan Bracksieck — Out of This World and Into Ourselves: Literature as a Bibliotherapeutic Journey (Houston Teachers Institute, 2004).** [Institutional teaching PDF](https://www.uh.edu/honors/resources-for-teachers/houston-teachers-institute/other-resources/pdfs/2004/beyond-houston---the-literature-of-travel-and-exploration/bracksieck.04.beyond-houston.pdf). Teaching/reference source; consulted SOURCE, THEME, MOOD AND TONE, not its cited books. Locators: PDF pp. 3–4 (printed pp. 21–22), SOURCE/THEME/MOOD AND TONE. Supports early rescue, Zaphod’s figurehead role, ship, Magrathea, cosmic computation and mice’s threat; corroborated with 7. Does not alone verify every late detail. Its broad psychological and bibliotherapy assertions are not adopted; no promise of therapy. Its wording “mice created the earth” is narrowed to commissioned construction in the digest.
6. **Carl R. Kropf — Douglas Adams’s “Hitchhiker” Novels as Mock Science Fiction (1988).** [Publisher’s openly available abstract](https://online.ucpress.edu/sfs/article-abstract/15/Part%201%20%2844%29/61/210369/Douglas-Adams-s-Hitchhiker-Novels-as-Mock-Science?redirectedFrom=fulltext). Review/scholarship; **abstract only**, full article not accessed. Locators: abstract’s conventional devices / frustrated expectations / chance / denial of closure sentences. Supports a critical position about the series; applying it to this first novel is labelled synthesis. No claim to have read pp. 61–70; those are publication pagination only.
7. **Novels for Students / Gale — The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy; Donna Woodford essay (1999).** [Public entry](https://www.encyclopedia.com/arts/educational-magazines/hitchhikers-guide-galaxy). Critical/reference source; consulted Plot Summary, Characters, Style, Critical Overview and the complete Donna Woodford essay under Criticism. Locators: Plot Summary subsections Earth, Hitchhiking, The Heart of Gold-2, Magrathea; Characters / Eddie and Marvin; Woodford paragraphs beginning with Kropf’s name, “The Hitchhiker’s Guide … is another example”, “Probably the best example”, and “While meaning and purpose”. Used for narrow late-event verification, critical disagreement and fallible knowledge. **The substantial criticism, not the tertiary synopsis, supplies the interpretive disagreement.** Richard Brown and Peter Kemp excerpts discuss later books: they were encountered but their later plots are not used. Errors in introductory radio chronology, “hold your breath” vacuum advice, beer-as-muscle-preparation and first-novel conflation are not repeated. Exact guard names, exact countdowns, invented dialogue, the alleged real physics, and surgical detail are omitted. Marvin’s effect on the police ship is reported only as the novel’s fictional explanation, not real mental-health causation.
8. **Neil Gaiman — Remembering Douglas (2003).** [Author’s own site](https://www.neilgaiman.com/Cool_Stuff/Essays/Introductions/Remembering_Douglas). Primary reminiscence; consulted full essay. Locators: opening meeting; drafts in the office; final “Futurologist, or an Explainer” paragraphs. Supports only an attributed portrait of Adams as a cross-media explainer; it is not evidence of the first novel’s plot or Adams’s intentions in 1979. Loose claims in the memoir about when computers/the Internet existed are not adopted.
