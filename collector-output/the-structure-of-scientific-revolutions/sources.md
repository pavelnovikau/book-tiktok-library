# Sources — The Structure of Scientific Revolutions

Access date: 2026-10-31. Author: Thomas S. Kuhn. Work: the 1962 essay and the enlarged second edition of 1970, including **Postscript—1969**. The date in that heading is not a claim that the second edition was published in 1969. This is an original bilingual condensation, not a published Russian translation.

## Edition and access boundary

The University of Colorado teaching selection includes the title page, the enlarged-second-edition imprint (copyright 1962, 1970; second edition, enlarged, 1970), contents, opening chapters and postscript. Its later reprint number line is visible; this is not asserted to be a first printing. Its 37 PDF pages are a selection, not the whole book. Printed pagination differs from PDF pagination.

The Illinois selection contains actual main-chapter pages but no imprint. Its exact printing is unidentified. The FGCU library-reserve selection explicitly credits chapter XIII to the 1996 third edition in its teaching annotation. These identities are kept separate rather than treating three excerpts as one complete 1970 copy. No textual collation of every passage across editions was performed. Ian Hacking's 2012 introductory essay and Kuhn's later writings are outside this condensation.

The scoped, read-only Apple Books search found no assigned-title/author match: 217 OPF files, 186 plist metadata files and one EPUB archive parsed without errors. No unrelated titles or filenames were recorded. Consequently there is no owned matching OPF/spine/body extent to certify. The lack of an owned EPUB did not decide the outcome: the retained argument was checked against actual institutional primary passages.

## What this supports and does not support

The retained sequence is normal science → expectation and anomaly → possible crisis and its alternative outcomes → revolutionary reconstruction → incommensurability and reasoned choice → progress without a guaranteed final ontology. The historical cases are Kuhn's cases, conveyed as his account, not a fresh historical or experimental validation. There are no invented scenes, dialogue, motives, examples, exercises or management applications.

This is selective primary reading. It is not continuous whole-book reading, a translation certificate, an independent scholarly edition, or exhaustive coverage of every historical case. Chapters IV–V and XI–XII were not read in full; relevant retained puzzle-solving, textbook-perspective and choice claims have direct support in the consulted passages identified above and in the postscript. No particular retained causal link is left dependent on a blurb, generic recap or memory.
