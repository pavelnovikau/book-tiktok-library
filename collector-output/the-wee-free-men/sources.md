## Claim/plot/theme map (before screens)

Desire: can someone ordinary, young and easily overlooked protect a home she understands? Problem: impressive appearances and inherited stories misidentify both danger and competence. Analytical thesis: this fantasy makes careful attention, revisable judgment and responsibility visible through a rescue adventure, without reducing those things to a spell or self-help command. Sequence: ordinary home disrupted → habits of seeing → competence in work and memory → dangerous stories and social judgment → an unruly alliance → rescue through unstable perception → narrative craft and limits → separately gated ending → invitation to the novel.

Distinct strands: epistemology; measuring/reading; skill and invisible labor; intergenerational memory; geography and stewardship; accusation and social authority; education; collaboration/consent; language and legal power; dreams and agency; mixed family feelings; comedy/translation; grief; outcome, recognition and credit. Book facts, independent criticism and this digest’s interpretations remain separate. Neither the narrator’s magic nor old sheep remedies are real scientific or health advice. Fantasy violence and clan customs are analyzed, never prescribed to a child. The fictional child is nine; 14+ describes the commissioned audience, not a publisher restriction or a recommendation to behave as she does.

Uncertainties narrowed: no unsupported scene-by-scene chronology; no linguistic claim that Tiffany truly has a Gaelic etymology; no clinical claim about layered thoughts; no assertion that a late vision proves literal resurrection; no claim that every community duty belongs to a child. Harper’s guide asks about the Queen’s final treatment but does not itself spell it out; the explicit outcome is therefore additionally attributed to review evidence. The literary idea of land as accumulated life has real geological scaffolding, not evidence for supernatural powers.

Original analysis and bilingual paraphrase only; no copied prose, dialogue, publisher art or source-book files in this package. No book media files are embedded; the maintained shell provides a neutral abstract cover and embeds licensed Geist/Geist Mono fonts.
