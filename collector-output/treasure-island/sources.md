# Treasure Island — source ledger and evidence boundary

Access date: 2026-10-31. Author: Robert Louis Stevenson. Audience: 14+. Delivery: original bilingual analytical digest, Russian default. No text from a published Russian translation is used. No edition cover, publisher artwork, illustrations or archival map image is reproduced. The cover is original typography, not a purported historical object.

## Narrative map used for the corpus

The organising desire is to go beyond the familiar shore. The tension is that a map can identify a destination without identifying whom to trust. The novel is treated as an adventure, not as a nonfiction thesis or a syllabus of life lessons.

Causal route: dangerous guest at the inn → papers recovered under threat → the map makes an expedition possible → recruitment brings the old pirate network aboard → Jim’s overhearing changes the balance of information → island geography and divided loyalties shape the struggle → the stockade creates a provisional refuge. The full continuation and ending are restricted to the Deep layer.

Principal characters retain different functions: Jim observes and acts without complete knowledge; Bones brings the past into the home; Livesey combines care with judgement; Trelawney supplies resources and dangerous confidence; Smollett supplies unwelcome competence; Silver combines genuine charm, coercion, calculation and specific acts of help; Ben Gunn is an isolated person with his own agency, not just a clue dispenser. Gray’s choice prevents the crew from becoming fixed moral categories.

Craft is grounded in the actual reading experience: sound before sight, place-specific action, interrupted knowledge, first-person retrospect, the doctor’s temporary narration, material obstacles, and the transformation of familiar images by the ending. Fear, loyalty and greed are tensions within events. The synthesis is a way of reading changing impressions, not an attributed universal moral from Stevenson.

## Sources

**1. Robert Louis Stevenson, Treasure Island. Project Gutenberg eBook 120.**

- Direct text: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/120/120-h/120-h.htm
- Plain text consulted during research: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/120/120-0.txt
- Role: `book`; public-domain primary novel.
- Used for: events, chronology, character speech and conduct, narrative frame, physical details and ending; chapter locators I–XXXIV are attached to the relevant screens.
- Evidence boundary: the lawful complete text was available. Research consulted chapter openings/endings across all 34 chapters and targeted passages for consequential scenes, especially XI–XIV and XXVI–XXXIV. This is not a claim of a fresh line-by-line reading of the entire novel. Illustrations and editorial/publisher art in the HTML edition were not used. The corpus is paraphrase, not copied chapters; it makes no edition-specific page claims.
- Checks affecting the digest: Arrow’s disappearance is not promoted into a witnessed murder; Alan’s death is reported through what Jim hears/Silver says; Jim’s shots at Hands are not recast as a cool deliberate trick shot; Silver’s later life is not reported as known fact. Detailed late-story statements use chapter evidence rather than film memory.

**2. Robert Louis Stevenson, “My First Book: ‘Treasure Island’,” in Essays in the Art of Writing. Project Gutenberg eBook 492.**

- Direct text: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/492/492-h/492-h.htm#page111
- Role: `author`; public-domain primary testimony about composition.
- Used for: the map as a generative device, family participation, excluded female roles in the stated conception, interrupted composition, literary debts, reconstruction of the lost map, and transformation of an acquaintance’s qualities into Silver.
- Evidence boundary: the essay was read in full. It is retrospective testimony, not an independent diary. The essay does not name the acquaintance in the passage about Silver; the artifact consequently does not pretend that this passage independently establishes an identity. No claim that the lost original map survives.

**3. Robert Louis Stevenson, “A Gossip on Romance,” in Essays of Robert Louis Stevenson, edited by William Lyon Phelps. Project Gutenberg eBook 10761.**

- Evidence boundary: Stevenson’s essay is separated from Phelps’s introduction and notes. Connections to individual Treasure Island scenes are labelled interpretation, not a claim that the essay provides a definitive key to each scene. No long quotations are reproduced.

## Distribution and limits

Seven sources: one primary novel, two primary author essays and four institutional/specialist reference pages. Hosts: Project Gutenberg (3), Royal Museums Greenwich (2), Glasgow University Library (1), Robert Louis Stevenson Website (1). No commercial summary, film adaptation, generated search-result synopsis or newly published companion edition is used as plot authority.

Source attachments by screen: 1 → 85; 2 → 6; 3 → 2; 4 → 2; 5 → 2; 6 → 1; 7 → 2. Some screens have multiple sources; these are attachments, not independent corroboration counts. Five framing/navigation/final screens have no external factual claim and no source attachment.

Visible evidence categories: 51 plot, 28 interpretation, 5 historical context, 3 author testimony and 9 synthesis/framing screens (cover included). Under schema v2 these map to `book`, `corroboration` and `synthesis`; the more specific `evidenceKind` is displayed in the local reader. `sourceLocator` provides chapter/section references. Interpretation is not presented as historical fact or as a quotation of authorial intention.

Unresolved matters remain unresolved: exact in-world year/location; the complete provenance of the hoard; confirmed later fates not supplied by the narrator; psychological diagnoses; comparative accuracy of particular Russian translations; supposed real island identifications. The selection does not claim a survey of all scholarly readings of the novel.

The reader’s Sources dialog uses non-revealing notes. Following an external primary-text or essay link can expose spoilers: this limit is explicitly disclosed in both languages. Spoiler protection controls the reader’s interface, not inspection of the embedded JSON or deliberately opened source files.

Cold-review locator corrections applied before integration: `geography-makes-action` uses chapters I, X–XI and XIII–XXI; `women-at-edge` uses chapters IV and VII plus “My First Book”; `opening-ending-mirror` uses chapters I, VI, X and XXXIV. The `women-at-edge` screen now also names Silver’s wife’s work running the inn and identifies the novel’s racial description as prejudice in the text rather than a neutral fact.
