## Claim map — established before screen writing

Question/desire: understand how an adaptive emergency response can become a health burden without concluding that illness is a failure of attitude.

Thesis: anticipation can repeatedly activate physiological systems suited to short physical emergencies; repetition, failure to adapt or switch off, and inappropriate responses alter risk across interacting organs. Exposure, appraisal, resources, history and social arrangements shape this process. Stress is a modifier of many risks, not a universal sufficient cause.

Causal spine: perceived/actual demand → autonomic and hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal signals → mobilization of energy/circulation, altered investment in digestion/growth/reproduction and immune regulation → short-term advantage → repeated activation or inadequate recovery → organ-specific vulnerabilities and feedback loops → psychological/social variation → context-appropriate coping plus changing harmful conditions.

## Source ledger (IDs match story-data.json)

### 1. Robert M. Sapolsky — supplied Russian EPUB (book)

Edition-identifying URL: https://www.piter.com/product/pochemu-u-zebr-ne-byvaet-infarkta-psihologiya-stressa . Accessed 2026-10-31. The URL is **a bibliographic pointer**, not the source of the chapter text; actual evidence is the user-supplied EPUB above. Publisher promotional causality is not adopted. Supports all `book` screens, located by chapter and relevant subsection. Evidence boundaries: early-2000s scientific framing is retained as the author's account; dated AIDS, sex/gender, endocrine, genetics and intervention claims are not presented as current clinical guidance. Full English edition not consulted. Author's own baboon research is not independent replication.

### 2. NIDDK — Peptic Ulcers (reference)

https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/peptic-ulcers-stomach-ulcers . Accessed 2026-10-31; page reviewed September 2022. Supports prioritizing H. pylori and NSAIDs and cause-directed care. Does not quantify an independent psychological-stress causal effect. Book's experimental mucosal mechanisms are not substituted for clinical etiology.

### 3. Cohen et al. (2012), PNAS — Chronic stress, glucocorticoid receptor resistance, inflammation, and disease risk (primary)

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3341031/ ; DOI 10.1073/pnas.1118355109. Accessed 2026-10-31, abstract and article discussion. Two viral-challenge studies (276 and 79 volunteers) link stress-related glucocorticoid resistance to colds/local inflammation. Stress exposure was not randomized. Supports tissue sensitivity, not a claim that all stress illnesses share this proven causal pathway.

### 4. National Cancer Institute — Stress and Cancer (reference)

https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/coping/feelings/stress-fact-sheet . Accessed 2026-10-31. Distinguishes laboratory progression mechanisms, mixed human incidence findings and weak direct-survival evidence. Supports care for distress without asserting that attitude prevents cancer, causes relapse or determines survival.

### 5. Kivimäki et al. (2012), Lancet — Job strain as a risk factor for coronary heart disease (primary pooled analysis)

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22981903/ ; DOI 10.1016/S0140-6736(12)60994-5. Accessed 2026-10-31, abstract. Cohort data: 197,473 participants, age- and sex-adjusted association HR 1.23 (95% CI 1.10–1.37). Observational association, not an individual prediction or proof that stress reduction prevents infarction; conventional risk factors remain important.

### 6. NCCIH — Meditation and Mindfulness: Effectiveness and Safety (reference)

https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/meditation-and-mindfulness-effectiveness-and-safety . Accessed 2026-10-31. Supports possible symptom benefits, heterogeneous methods, quality limitations and potential adverse experiences. Does not support superiority of one commercial method, replacement of medical care or generalized disease prevention.

### 7. WHO (2022) — WHO guidelines on mental health at work (reference)

https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK586381/ ; official overview https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/9789240053052 . Accessed 2026-10-31, recommendations. Supports attention to psychosocial conditions and both organizational and individual measures. Recommendations are often conditional with low/very-low certainty; no promise of a universal intervention effect.

### 8. NIMH — Depression (reference)

https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/depression . Accessed 2026-10-31. Supports multifactorial causes and individualized psychotherapy/medication care; persistent symptoms warrant assessment. Does not support diagnosing depression from cortisol or replacing treatment with willpower. No medication doses or self-treatment instructions are given.

### 9. NIDDK — Insulin Resistance & Prediabetes (reference)

https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/what-is-diabetes/prediabetes-insulin-resistance . Accessed 2026-10-31. Supports tissue insulin resistance and multiple risk factors. Does not establish psychological stress as a necessary or sufficient diabetes cause. No book-era prevalence values retained.

### 10. NICHD — Cushing Syndrome (reference)

https://www.nichd.nih.gov/health/topics/cushing . Accessed 2026-10-31; reviewed May 9, 2019. Supports pathological prolonged glucocorticoid exposure from medicines/tumors and complex diagnosis. A clinical endocrine syndrome is not equivalent to everyday worry. General NIDDK Cushing page fetch timed out and was not used as evidence.

### 11. Gilbertson et al. (2002), Nature Neuroscience — Smaller hippocampal volume predicts pathologic vulnerability to psychological trauma (primary)

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12379862/ ; DOI 10.1038/nn958. Accessed 2026-10-31, abstract. Twin design supports a pre-existing vulnerability interpretation for some PTSD-associated volume differences. Does not show that acquired stress-related changes never occur; does not establish neuron counts from MRI.

### 12. Border et al. (2019), American Journal of Psychiatry — No Support for Historical Candidate Gene or Candidate Gene-by-Interaction Hypotheses for Major Depression Across Multiple Large Samples (primary)

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30845820/ ; DOI 10.1176/appi.ajp.2018.18070881. Accessed 2026-10-31, abstract. Challenges prominent candidate-gene hypotheses across large samples. Does not negate polygenic inheritance, environmental effects or every possible gene–environment interaction.

### 13. Sapolsky & Share (2004), PLOS Biology — A Pacific Culture among Wild Baboons: Its Emergence and Transmission (primary, author-linked)

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC387274/ ; DOI 10.1371/journal.pbio.0020106. Accessed 2026-10-31, abstract and discussion. One troop's unusually affiliative patterns persisted after male turnover following selective tuberculosis mortality. A natural observation, not randomized social reform or independent corroboration of the author's own work. Used with an explicit author-linked label.

## Evidence decisions and exclusions

- Disease mechanisms described as the book's argument remain `book`, even when biologically plausible. A source badge is not an endorsement of every claim as settled contemporary medicine.
- Animal manipulations can establish animal mechanisms; human cohorts carry residual confounding, selection and reverse-causation possibilities. These evidence classes are not collapsed.
- Current-source checks are selective safeguards, not a systematic review through 2026. Access date means consulted then, not publication then.
- No percentages for stress-caused insomnia/ulcers, no deterministic prenatal predictions, no universal cortisol pattern, no stress-cancer causal claim, no medication or hormone recommendation.
- Human sex-related statements describe the reproductive physiology discussed by the author and are not universal claims about identity or every individual.
- Modern source notes and screens are deliberately concise. No external source is mined for long translated passages. All bodies are original paraphrases; no quotations are necessary.
- Health caveat appears on the cover, in method disclosure and in treatment-sensitive screens: educational analysis, **not medical advice, diagnosis or treatment**. Persistent or severe symptoms require professional assessment; urgent symptoms require urgent care. No treatment should be changed on the basis of this digest.
- No source book, extracted chapters, remote assets, network runtime or deployment is included. Shared catalogue/manifest/service-worker files are outside scope.
