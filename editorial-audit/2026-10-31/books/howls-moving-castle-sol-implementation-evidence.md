2026-10-31. Scope: the 1986 Diana Wynne Jones novel. This is an implementation source map, with explicit spoilers, for a new queued package. It is not an independent review or a certificate of full-book fidelity.

Source IDs resolve to direct URLs and consulted scopes in `collector-output/howls-moving-castle/sources.md` and the identical reader source records. ## Identity and lawful access

- Primary sample: HarperCollins, ISBN 9780007380459, distributed as a legal Litres excerpt. Accessible chapter-one prose was read. Its publisher/rights metadata is distinct from the library’s 2001 reprint record.
- Bibliography: the indexed Kalamazoo Public Library record identifies the 1986 Greenwillow original and a 2001 HarperTrophy reprint. Direct opening returned 403; no claim of reading the library’s 329-page edition is made.
- Local Apple Books: 216 expanded EPUB directories, 216 readable OPFs, no parse failures and no assigned-title metadata match. There is therefore no inspected candidate spine/body extent to certify. No unrelated title or file path is included.
- Narrative evidence: accessible LitCharts chapter-event accounts for chapters 1–21, public character entries/quoted passages, and the public SuperSummary account of chapters 17–21. Those are substantial accountable guides, not the primary novel. Primary anchors were read selectively through the legal excerpt and quoted Jones passages; the whole book was not read continuously.

## Fidelity decisions and uncertainty

1. Sophie acts throughout: leaves, obtains shelter, bargains, cooks/cleans, helps Michael, visits the King, attempts the dog-man’s cure, confronts Howl, goes to the Waste, commands the stick, saves Calcifer and restores Howl. Howl does not replace her as the rescuer of Calcifer.
2. The contract is not a simple ownership pact or a cartoon power source. Its public restrictions are established early; its origin, heart dependency and safe dissolution stay behind Deep consent.
3. Pentstemmon’s concern and Howl’s statement about Sophie sustaining her disguise are attributed. The sequence shows Sophie’s youth returning but does not diagnose its unique psychological cause. No “believe in yourself” rule, management analogy or authorial moral was added.
4. A public Angorian character introduction loosely assigns the Witch’s death to the demon. The chapter-21 event accounts instead describe Howl’s magic defeating her at the fortress, followed by the separate defeat of the demon at home. The retelling uses the explicit sequence corroborated by both late-chapter guides; it does not silently adopt the character entry’s compressed attribution.
5. Excluded: Miyazaki’s war, bombers, bird transformations, film Suliman, a prince restored by a kiss, time-travel encounter with young Howl, film colour changes at the ending and sequel events. The novel’s male Suliman and mixed body-part problem are kept distinct.
6. Exact print pagination, full-edition wording, omitted subplots and comparison with a specific Russian translation remain outside the evidence boundary. No copyright text or private EPUB contents are stored in the repository.
