# Hard to Be a God — expanded pilot source ledger

Candidate only, authored 2026-10-31 / 2026-11-01. Status: ready-for-independent-check, subject to the completed implementation checks in QA.md. No independent literary PASS is issued.

Work: Arkady and Boris Strugatsky, «Трудно быть богом» / Hard to Be a God. The sole narrative authority is the complete Russian electronic witness on the official author site: https://www.rusf.ru/abs/books/tbb100.htm . It is the novella itself, not a chapter guide, film account, critical essay or summary. No particular printed edition or textual variant is certified.

It is byte-identical to the specified, read-only private witness in `book-tiktok-editorial-audit-20261031/group-4-private/`. The bytes were decoded as cp1251, not mistaken for UTF-8. Retrieval and hash verification were not counted as reading. Neither full primary text nor a primary-text extraction was written into this candidate or the public repository. The read helper only emits requested ranges from the existing private file in memory.

Actual personal reading covered every nonempty paragraph in the prologue, all ten chapters and epilogue, in successive bounded chapter chunks: **1,754 narrative paragraphs**. Headings and front epigraphs were also inspected. Three initial displays clipped a middle range; each omitted range was reread before writing: prologue P090–P116, chapter 2 P012–P025 and chapter 5 P008–P016. `source-coverage.json` records the actual continuous chunks, section counts, normalized section hashes and these rereads. This pilot therefore has a broader actual reading boundary than the old collector repairs; no old reviewer’s partial reading is silently promoted to complete reading.

Locators are explicitly **D2:<source division>:Pnnn**: HTMLParser with character references decoded, nonempty `p` elements in document order after each `p.zg1` section heading, inline text joined and whitespace normalized. These are electronic-witness paragraph ordinals, not print pages. They happen to match many pass-2 ordinals but are independently labelled. Every narrative screen has its own locator in story-data.json / scene-map.json. `chapter-scene-map.md` maps all 91 narrative beats to the connected drafts and source passages. The global Sources dialog provides the source map for the selected depth; it does not expose hidden Deep titles before consent.

The Russian connected draft was written before segmentation. Its complete English translation was written next; only then were the paragraphs assigned to nested routes. Two mixed paragraphs were divided at changes of scene so lower routes can retain the home/camera transition without disclosing a Deep scene. No narrative words were added by segmentation. The authoring drafts contain the full plot and are working review artifacts, not spoiler-safe entry pages.

The six repaired causal/epistemic hinges remain:

- Arata’s explicit human targets and promise to return the weapons, Rumata’s lesser spoken mortality objection, his separate private land → serfs → new barons forecast, and his felt inferiority: chapter 9 D2:P023–P052; candidate `arata-lightning` through `arata-leaves`.
- Okana’s attraction is the means Rumata considers using to obtain information; he leaves without completing the encounter. The later drunken return, Uno’s protective lie, confession and brine response are retained: chapter 2 P023, chapter 4 P022–P053 and P179–P218; `letters`, `okana-departure`, `drinking-night`, `uno-vigil`, `kira-confession`. Deep also restores the intervening news about Okana.
- Actual Budakh release: useless procedural sheet, bracelets from the chest, direct prison visit, claimed gift from the bishop, monks delivering the physician, removal of the noose: chapter 8 P059–P124; `clearance`, `prison-bluff`, `budakh-release`. No completed circuit of administrative offices is invented.
- Final chronology: the real ending precedes a neutral reading-route screen. It does not reopen the search or protection tasks in the present tense. `final` is interface framing, not a moral or a renewed episode.
- Capture: dropped axes, dislocated arms, spear impact, crowd and net, without an invented weapon-breaking action: chapter 6 P023–P033; `capture`, `prince-bedroom`. Pampa’s own source-supported beam/table cutting occurs in other scenes and is not transferred to this fight.
- Violence after Kira: no identified individual shooter, choreographed revenge expedition, victim number, certain account of each death, announced cure or settled political future. Pashka’s incomplete account and the berry-juice scene carry the ending: chapter 10 P083–P103, epilogue P001–P037; `attack` through `strawberries`. The imagined Earth journey and proposed castle refuge remain uncompleted plans. Pashka’s highway association remains his uncertain impression.

Material compression remains intentional. The source is much longer than this 7,825-Russian-word narrative. Omitted or substantially shortened: much of the children's banter and Deva Katya episode; most forest legends, saint lore, minor genealogies and country history; the pot-cart street farce and several incidental meals, duels and court jokes; numerous secondary career/political details of Reba; graphic torture catalogues; the full theoretical night meditation; precise histories and troop totals of Arata’s earlier wars; and much repetitive court ceremony. These omissions do not remove a source division, the principal relationship arc, either central argument, the actual release mechanism, Condor’s change of position or the real ending. Independent review should still judge whether these choices preserve a sustained reading experience.
