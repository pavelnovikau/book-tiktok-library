## Causal spine

1. Anticipation recruits a protective response; prolonged activity, failed shutoff and deferred repair create costs.
2. Neural/endocrine communication, receptors and feedback explain how appraisal reaches distinct organs.
3. Circulation, energy, appetite, gut, development, reproduction and immunity demonstrate different costs and conditions.
4. Pain, memory, sleep and aging introduce active processing, timing, reciprocal loops and preserved function.
5. Experiments vary information, outlets and control while holding physical exposure similar.
6. Depression, temperament and reward explain heterogeneous vulnerability, learned expectations and persistent seeking.
7. Hierarchy, material resources, inequality and public systems determine who encounters chronic demands and recovery options.
8. Flexible coping fits actual controllability; individual strategies and changes in social conditions remain necessary at different scales.

### 1. Anticipation, allostasis, and the price of mobilization

A response useful during an immediate physical emergency can be initiated by anticipated events. Damage often comes from its persistent operation, failed shutoff, or mismatched recovery, rather than a stock of stress hormones running out.

### 2. Neural and endocrine communication

The brain's appraisal reaches organs through identifiable neural and chemical routes, with different speeds, receptors and feedback. There is no single fixed hormonal signature of every stressor.

Mechanisms: Sympathetic signaling, norepinephrine and adrenal-medullary epinephrine provide a rapid route. Hypothalamic CRH → pituitary ACTH → adrenal-cortical glucocorticoids; feedback limits secretion. Pituitary stimulation and inhibition, tissue sensitivity, timing, species and context complicate a simple command model.

### 4. Fuel storage, mobilization and cumulative burden

Insulin supports storage; emergency signals mobilize and redistribute fuel. Repeated reversal has costs and can aggravate metabolic vulnerability, while combined modest disturbances can matter across systems.

Mechanisms: Glycogen, triglycerides and protein connect circulating nutrients with reserves. Stress restricts storage, promotes glucose production and changes insulin effectiveness in different tissues. Type 1 autoimmune insulin deficiency differs from type 2 resistance and compensatory secretion; later beta-cell decline is not conversion into autoimmune type 1.

### 5. Appetite, fat distribution, gut and ulcer defenses

Timing explains opposite appetite responses, while different gut regions and mucosal defenses respond differently. Infection changes the ulcer explanation without answering why every infected person has the same outcome.

Visceral fat, insulin and reward-related eating complicate a simple calories-versus-willpower account. Upper-tract inhibition, colonic activity and visceral sensitivity differ. Mucus, flow, repair, acid rebound and ischemia/reperfusion provide distinct ulcer pathways.

### 6. Development, active contact and growth

Mechanisms: Prenatal conditions can alter later metabolic fit and HPA-feedback sensitivity. Growth requires coordinated GH, somatomedin and other signals; acute metabolic GH action differs from long-term growth action. Bones continue remodeling after childhood; strong glucocorticoid exposure and everyday stress are different evidence conditions.

### 8. Immune timing, clinical illness, cancer and mistaken normality

The immune system is regulated in both directions. Acute mobilization, later restraint, persistent suppression and inadequate restraint differ. A plausible immune pathway is insufficient evidence for infection, spontaneous cancer or survival claims.

Selection of the reference population can create a fictitious anatomical abnormality.
